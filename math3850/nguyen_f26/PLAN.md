## Logan Chapter 1 → Haberman map

Give students this table with the SpringerLink URL. Logan's Chapter 1 is the strongest
part of that book and is exactly the part where Haberman is thinnest — where the
equations come from.

| Logan | Haberman counterpart | What Logan adds |
|---|---|---|
| §1.1 PDE Models | §1.1 + Ch. 1 intro | Broad survey; plane waves, dispersion relations |
| §1.2 Conservation Laws | §1.2 (and §12.6.2 traffic flow) | **The general flux/constitutive framework.** Haberman derives heat conduction specifically; Logan derives the template that heat, advection, and traffic flow all instantiate |
| §1.3 Diffusion | §1.2–1.3 | Fick's law alongside Fourier's; diffusion time scale; groundwater and contaminant examples |
| §1.4 Diffusion and Randomness | **no counterpart** | Random-walk derivation of the heat equation. Worth assigning — it is the one derivation students remember |
| §1.5 Vibrations and Acoustics | §4.2 | Same string derivation, plus acoustics and gas dynamics |
| §1.6 Quantum Mechanics* | **no counterpart** | Optional; Schrödinger equation. Starred in Logan too |
| §1.7 Heat Conduction in Higher Dimensions | §1.5 | Same divergence-theorem derivation, more compact |
| §1.8 Laplace's Equation | §1.4 (steady state), §2.5.4 | Harmonic functions, maximum principle, Dirichlet's principle up front rather than as an afterthought |
| §1.9 Classification of PDEs | **no counterpart** | Elliptic / parabolic / hyperbolic via the discriminant. Haberman never does this systematically — a real gap worth filling in one lecture |

### Beyond Chapter 1, if you want it

- Logan §2.1–2.4 (Cauchy problems, heat kernel, d'Alembert, well-posedness,
  semi-infinite domains) is a cleaner treatment than Haberman §12.3–12.5 and is free.
  Good optional reading alongside meetings 18–19.
- Logan §3.2 develops Fourier series as orthogonal expansion in an inner-product space
  (L², Bessel's inequality, completeness). If your students can take it, it makes
  Haberman Ch. 5 land much harder. Optional reading alongside meetings 20–22.
- Logan §4.7 (sources, Duhamel's principle) pairs directly with Haberman Ch. 8 in
  Version B.

### Suggested student-facing framing

> Logan is a free optional supplement, not a second required text. Read §1.2, §1.3,
> and §1.4 in the first two weeks — Logan explains where these equations come from
> better than any other undergraduate source. Everything you are graded on comes from
> Haberman and from class.
